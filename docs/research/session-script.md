# Session Script: First-Time Play on a Phone

Use this script word for word so sessions are comparable. The session lasts about 30 minutes. The moderator observes and takes notes, and does not teach.

## Recruiting

- **Participants:** 10–15 people who have never played CaseGrid.
- **Spread of puzzle experience:** aim for roughly a third each:
  - **Low:** rarely plays puzzles.
  - **Medium:** plays casual puzzles such as Wordle or crosswords.
  - **High:** plays logic-grid puzzles, Sudoku, or deduction games regularly.
- **Device:** the participant's own phone and usual browser.
- **Exclusions:** people who have worked on CaseGrid, or who have seen its cases.

## Before the session

1. Clear site data for the test URL on the participant's phone, or use a fresh private tab.
2. Record the device model, browser, and orientation in the observation log. Record the viewport width too if you can read it from remote developer tools. Otherwise look it up later from the device model.
3. Start a stopwatch the moment the participant first sees the home screen.

## Consent (read aloud)

> "Thanks for helping. We're testing the game, not you, so there are no wrong answers. I'll take notes on what happens on screen, but I won't record your name or anything that identifies you. Please think out loud as you go. You can stop at any time."

## Task 1: First mystery (up to 12 minutes)

> "Please open this link and play however you'd normally play a new game. Keep talking about what you're thinking."

Give no further instructions. If the participant asks a question, reply: **"What would you try?"** Give direct help only if they are stuck for **3 minutes** or say they would quit. Log each intervention with its timestamp and what you said.

Observe and note:

- Which entry point they choose first, such as "Solve your first mystery", a case card, or something else.
- Whether they read the briefing, how long it takes, and whether they skip or follow the onboarding coach.
- The time of their **first meaningful placement**: the first placement they justify out loud with a clue.
- Every stall longer than 30 seconds, and its cause. Classify each one:
  - **Reasoning:** they understand the controls but cannot see the deduction.
  - **Control:** they know what they want to do but the interface blocks or confuses them.
  - **Discovery:** they do not know a feature exists, such as notes, nudges, or undo.
- Every nudge they open, and whether it helped.
- Whether they finish **unaided**: no moderator help. Nudges are allowed and recorded separately.

## Task 2: After the case (up to 5 minutes)

If they closed the case:

> "What would you do now?"

Note whether they read the resolution, open "See the deductions", share, open the next case, or leave. If they start a second case, let them play for up to 3 minutes and note it.

If they did not finish:

> "Where would you go from here?"

## Task 3: Sharing (up to 3 minutes, growth release only)

> "Suppose you wanted a friend to try this case. Show me how you'd do it."

Note what they expect a share to contain, whether they worry about spoilers, and whether the share completes, is cancelled, or fails.

## Debrief (5 minutes)

1. "In one sentence, what is this game about?"
2. "What was the most satisfying moment? What was the most frustrating?"
3. "Was there anything you wanted to do but couldn't?"
4. "How likely are you to play again this week, and why?" Record their own words; do not use a numeric score.
5. "May we send you one follow-up question in seven days?" Record yes or no only. Keep contact details outside this repository.

## Seven-day follow-up (for those who agreed)

> "Since our session, have you opened CaseGrid again? If so, what brought you back, and did you start or finish a case? Did you send a case to anyone, and do you know whether they played it?"

Record answers as self-reported. They cannot be verified.

## After each session

Complete [observation-log-template.md](observation-log-template.md) the same day, while the notes are fresh. File it as `docs/research/sessions/PNN.md`.

## Hypotheses to test

These were written **before** any sessions, from the case audit (`docs/content/case-audit.md`) and a review of the UI. They are predictions to check, not findings. Confirm, revise, or discard each one after the baseline.

| # | Hypothesis | Sibling story | What would disconfirm it |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | New players do not know where to begin in a catalog of 20 cards; a single "Solve your first mystery" entry point shortens the time to the first placement. | #16 | Most participants open case 001 quickly without the entry point. |
| H2 | Learning adjacency, blocked cells, and notes on the real case-001 board, with skippable steps, reduces control and discovery stalls. | #16 | Stalls remain mostly control or discovery, or players skip the coach and still do well. |
| H3 | Players who reach CASE CLOSED do not know what to do next; an "Open the next case" action raises second-case starts. | #17 | Closers already start a second case from the catalog. |
| H4 | An authored resolution and a walkthrough make the win feel earned. Players read them, and the walkthrough settles any doubt. | #17 | Players skip both, or say the ending felt flat anyway. |
| H5 | Accidental taps on phones cause frustration; undo removes it. | #18 | No accidental placements are observed. |
| H6 | Stuck players give up rather than guess; optional nudges keep them going without spoiling the solve. | #19 | Stuck players ignore nudges, or say nudges gave the answer away. |
| H7 | Players spot the "person beside the victim is the murderer" pattern by the second case, which makes later cases feel predictable. | #15 | No participant notices or uses the pattern. |
| H8 | Some players want to challenge a friend if the share reveals nothing about the solution. | #20 | No participant wants to share, or they worry the share spoils the case. |

# Cabinet Portraits

The circle holds a small, self-contained character study. Broad shoulders ground each composition while the head, hair, and headwear establish its silhouette. Meticulously crafted contours carry the identity before a viewer notices the smaller marks. Negative space remains open around the face so that every expression survives reduction.

Color behaves like ink printed onto warm stock: restrained, opaque, and slightly mineral. Verdigris, navy, olive, plum, and ochre bring variety without competing for attention. A single shadow plane gives each face volume. Painstaking attention to the balance of skin, cloth, and background keeps the collection cohesive without turning its people into interchangeable color variants.

Costume tells a compact story. A raised loupe, a peaked cap, a braid, or a feather becomes the principal identifying gesture; smaller occupational details reward closer inspection. This hierarchy is the product of careful craft: a shape must first read at a glance, then invite a second look. Expressions suggest temperament without announcing guilt.

Fine charcoal outlines and sparse internal strokes evoke an archival illustration cabinet. The outer ring and pale inner keyline provide a steady rhythm across the collection. The review sheet places generous portraits beside true-size miniatures, with quiet serif names and monospaced catalog numbers. Its purpose is to expose proportion, legibility, and consistency, making the final refinement as deliberate as the first drawing.
